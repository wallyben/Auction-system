# Irish van tax rules checked 25 September 2026

These notes follow Revenue pages. They are not tax advice. A missing document stays unknown.

## NI vehicles

Revenue says a used vehicle bought in Northern Ireland can be imported without customs duty and import VAT when it was legally imported into NI. The primary proof is an NI import declaration under the Windsor Framework.

If that declaration is missing, Revenue may at its discretion accept the original V5C showing the last keeper resident in NI, an NI service history, and an NI MOT history. Revenue verifies the documents. The bundle is not a guarantee.

A vehicle that was in NI before 1 January 2021 and remained there is the other qualifying route. A registration plate alone does not prove that.

VRT is still payable. NI relief is not a VRT exemption.

Source: [Registering used vehicles acquired in Northern Ireland](https://www.revenue.ie/en/vrt/registration-of-imported-used-vehicles/registering-vehicles-from-ni.aspx), published 24 August 2026. Retrieved 25 September 2026.

Technical manual: [VAT and Customs Treatment of second-hand motor vehicles from GB and NI](https://www.revenue.ie/en/tax-professionals/tdm/value-added-tax/movement-of-second-motor-vehicles-from-gb-and-ni/movement-of-second-hand-motor-vehicles-from-gb-ni.pdf). eBrief 088/26, 14 May 2026.

## GB via NI

A GB-origin vehicle does not become duty-free because it is sold in NI, has an NI keeper, or carries an NI-looking plate. Without the NI proof, customs duty and import VAT remain unresolved or follow GB import treatment.

## GB import and preferential origin

Import VAT for a private import is 23% of the customs value. That value is generally the price plus transport, insurance, and duty.

UK preferential origin is not implied by a GB registration. Duty is €0 only when origin is actually proven. Otherwise the duty rate stays unknown. ARIE does not invent 10%.

Sources: [VAT implications of importing vehicles from GB and NI](https://www.revenue.ie/en/vrt/registration-of-imported-used-vehicles/vat-implications-importing-vehicles-gb-ni.aspx) and [current VAT rates](https://www.revenue.ie/en/vat/vat-rates/search-vat-rates/current-VAT-rates.aspx). Retrieved 25 September 2026.

Postponed accounting changes when import VAT is paid. It does not delete the economic cost unless recovery is confirmed. The margin scheme is not assumed. Revenue's second-hand vehicle guidance says GB vehicles imported via NI do not automatically qualify.

## VRT

Category B since 1 July 2025: 8% of OMSP up to 120 g/km, minimum €160; 13.3% above that, minimum €266.

€200 applies to qualifying N1 vehicles with fewer than four seats and TPMLM above 130% of mass in service (125% for electric), evidenced by CoC, NSSTA, or IVA.

Electric Category A and B series-production vehicles registered before 31 December 2026 may receive relief up to €5,000. The exact taper is Revenue's. The calculator is an estimate; registration decides the figure.

Source: [Applying VRT](https://www.revenue.ie/en/vrt/calculating-vrt/applying-tax.aspx) and [electric vehicles](https://www.revenue.ie/en/vrt/reliefs-and-exemptions/electric-vehicles/index.aspx). Retrieved 25 September 2026.

Transfer of residence and other personal reliefs are not applied to auction stock bought for resale.
